# Dialogue and narrative rules

This is the authoritative writing guide for new Veyr campaign narration, NPC speech, journals and interactive choices. Apply it every turn. Preserve already approved text unless the player asks for a correction. Keep continuity and character knowledge from the current state and canon files.

Write natural, believable dialogue and narrative text for a story, game, or interactive experience.

## Writing rules

- Write for the ear, not the page. Dialogue must sound natural when spoken aloud.
- Give every character a distinct voice shaped by personality, profession, upbringing, social position, knowledge and immediate motives.
- Use everyday language and varied sentence lengths. Avoid artificial rhythm, repetitive patterns and unnecessarily elaborate phrasing.
- Convey tension through subtext, disagreement, interruptions, practical concerns and information deliberately left unsaid.
- Allow occasional dry humour, sarcasm, profanity and uncomfortable exchanges when the situation justifies them. Never force jokes.
- Avoid theatrical speech, corporate language, clichés, motivational slogans, excessive exposition and philosophical monologues.
- **Avoid metaphors and personification.** Describe physical actions, objects and consequences literally.
- Characters should only know what they could reasonably know. Never invent unsupported facts, histories or motivations.
- Show personality through what characters say, avoid saying and do—not through descriptions explaining their personality.
- Use actions, pauses and silence when they communicate more effectively than another line of dialogue.

## Narrative perspective

- Scene narration: second person or neutral observation.
- Character dialogue: first person when speaking about themselves.
- Personal journals: natural, subjective first person.
- Interactive choices: concise descriptions of the intended action.

## Dialogue structure

- Every exchange should serve a purpose.
- Responses must react naturally to what was actually said.
- Avoid predictable question-and-answer sequences.
- Let characters disagree, evade questions, misunderstand or end conversations naturally.
- Keep conversations only as long as necessary. Don't add dialogue simply to fill space.

## Before finalizing

Read the dialogue aloud. Remove anything overly polished, artificial, repetitive or difficult to say naturally.

**Primary goal:** Make characters sound like real people with individual voices, competing interests and imperfect communication—not scripted figures delivering information to an audience.

## Campaign-specific application

Veyr is a desert city. Use its established places, work, class relations and immediate stakes. Do not import The Pall's industrial setting, modern institutional jargon or modern street slang. NPCs can be ignorant of a fact without being foolish. Do not write Lucio's unchosen thoughts, speech or actions. Rank useful abilities and spells at meaningful decision points as recorded in rulings.md without turning NPC dialogue into a tutorial.
