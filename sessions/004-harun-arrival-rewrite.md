# Harun yard arrival — authorized rewrite
Real date: 2026-11-20. In-world: Day 3 ~15:36. Replaces prior arrival/voice/summoning response at player request; stats and earlier events unchanged.

You pass over the West Gate and slow above the caravan yard.

Below, a handler has a mule backed between two loaded carts. He notices the black fume descending past the roof and lets the lead slip through his fingers. The mule turns sharply. A man beside him catches the rope before the animal can bolt.

Then he sees you too.

“Inside,” he says to the handler. “Go on.”

Across the yard, Harun steps out of his office. For a moment he stares upward with his mouth open. He retreats into the doorway, catches his shoulder against the frame, then comes halfway out again.

You hover level with the awning. Nothing supports you. The smoke gathers into arms, separates, and gathers again.

Someone near the gate begins to run.

Then you speak.

“Harun. Find Yasin. Bring him here.”

The first words sound directly above Harun. His name sounds again beside the stable wall. By the end of the command, the muffled voice is behind the men at the carts.

The man holding the mule rope turns so quickly it pulls tight across his palm. He lets out a short cry and drops to one knee, still hanging on. A worker crouched behind a cart presses both hands over his ears.

Harun looks into his empty office. Then at you.

“Yasin?”

He barely gets the name out.

“Upstairs. He’s upstairs.”

He points, but his arm shakes. He lowers it and grips his wrist with his other hand.

One of the workers whispers, “Don’t call him down.”

Harun hears. He looks at the man, then at the loft.

“Get behind the stable.”

The worker stays crouched.

“Now.”

Harun crosses toward the stairs. He keeps several yards between himself and you, and stops well short of the bottom step.

“Yasin!”

No answer.

Harun swallows. His next shout is much louder.

“Come down!”

The loft door opens.

Short dark beard. Scar through the left eyebrow. Blue shirt. The man steps outside and takes the rail. When he starts down, you see the wire holding his right boot together.

He stops in the sunlight.

The steps cast dark bars against the stable wall. His body casts nothing.

Harun has been watching his face. Now he follows your line of sight toward the ground. He looks at the man’s boots, then at his own shadow stretching toward the stairs.

He backs away.

“Harun,” the man says. “What is that?”

Harun doesn’t answer.

The man looks from him to the black fume above the yard. His grip loosens on the rail.

“Was it asking for me?”

Harun takes another step back. This time the man notices where he is looking.

He goes still.
